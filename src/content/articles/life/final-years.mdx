Following the collapse of the <GlossaryTerm id="rajneeshpuram" text="Rajneeshpuram" /> commune in late 1985, Osho faced criminal charges from the United States government.

On November 14, 1985, <Claim level="established" cite={["usdoj-cr85-0150"]}>Osho entered an Alford plea to two counts of immigration fraud</Claim>. Under an <GlossaryTerm id="alford-plea" text="Alford plea" />, a defendant does not admit guilt but acknowledges that the prosecution has enough evidence to secure a conviction. He received a 10-year suspended sentence, was fined $400,000, and agreed to leave the United States immediately <Cite id="oregon-ag-investigation" />.

## The Global Exile

After being deported from the US, Osho embarked on a global tour seeking a new country to establish a commune. However, the notoriety of Rajneeshpuram preceded him.

<Claim level="established" cite={['britannica-osho']}>
  Over the course of 1986, Osho was denied entry, had visas revoked, or was deported by 21 different
  countries
</Claim>
, including Greece, Switzerland, Sweden, Great Britain, and Ireland.

During this exile, Osho framed himself as a martyr to truth, publishing _Socrates Poisoned Again After 25 Centuries_ (1986). He claimed that global governments were conspiring against him because his teachings threatened their religious and political control <Cite id="osho-socrates-poisoned" />.

## Return to Pune

Unable to find a permanent home abroad, Osho returned to India in July 1986. By January 1987, he had resettled at the original ashram in Koregaon Park, Pune.

The ashram was rebranded as the "Osho International Meditation Resort," pivoting away from the chaotic and controversial encounter therapies of the 1970s toward a more structured, polished, and commercially viable model <Cite id="urban-2015" />.

During these final years, Osho's health steadily declined. He often appeared frail during his evening discourses. In February 1989, he dropped the title "Bhagwan Shree Rajneesh" and officially adopted the name "Osho," derived from William James' concept of "oceanic experience" <Cite id="joshi-1982" />.

> Death is not an end. It is only the drop disappearing into the ocean. Do not mourn for me; celebrate! If you have loved me, continue to meditate, continue to celebrate. My presence will be felt wherever people sit in silence together.

## Death and the Question of Accountability

In April 1989, Osho entered a period of public silence. On January 19, 1990, at the age of 58, Osho died at the Pune ashram.

<Claim level="established" cite={['britannica-osho']}>
  The official cause of death reported by his physician, Dr. Gokul Gokani, was heart failure
  (massive coronary thrombosis).
</Claim>

Some of his followers immediately propagated theories that he had been secretly poisoned by the US government while in custody in 1985, a claim Osho himself had suggested before his death. However, no independent medical evidence has ever substantiated this <Claim level="alleged" cite={["osho-socrates-poisoned"]}>allegation</Claim>.

Osho died without ever publicly apologizing or taking direct responsibility for the harm caused by his movement. While he expelled Sheela and blamed her for the crimes in Oregon, he never acknowledged his own role in fostering the culture of absolute obedience and isolation that made those crimes possible <Cite id="carter-2011" />.

His epitaph, inscribed on his samadhi in Pune, reads:  
> *"OSHO. Never Born, Never Died. Only Visited this Planet Earth between Dec 11 1931 – Jan 19 1990."*
