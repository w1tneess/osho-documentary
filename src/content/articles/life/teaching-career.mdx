Following his graduation in 1957, Osho joined Raipur Sanskrit College as a teaching assistant. However, his tenure was brief. The Vice-Chancellor, viewing his unorthodox ideas and charismatic influence as disruptive to the students' moral education, asked him to transfer <Cite id="carter-2011" />.

In 1958, he began lecturing in philosophy at Jabalpur University. Over the next eight years, he established himself as an extraordinarily popular but highly controversial lecturer. 

<Claim level="established" cite={["joshi-1982"]}>He drew large crowds of students and colleagues, impressing them with his vast reading, rhetorical skill, and willingness to critique any established dogma.</Claim>

## The Traveling Acharya

During his tenure at Jabalpur University, Osho (then known as Acharya Rajneesh) began traveling extensively throughout India. He organized "Jivan Jagruti Andolan" (Life Awakening Movement) and regularly gave public lectures in major cities. 

His talks were fiery and iconoclastic. <Claim level="established" cite={["carter-2011", "urban-2015"]}>He criticized orthodox Indian religions, the political establishment, and particularly Mahatma Gandhi, whom he viewed as a champion of poverty and regression.</Claim> Osho argued that India needed capitalism, science, and technology to escape its material misery, not asceticism <Cite id="urban-2015" />.

## From Sex to Superconsciousness

In 1968, Osho delivered a series of five talks at the Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan in Mumbai. These talks, later published as *From Sex to Superconsciousness*, cemented his notoriety. 

He argued that sexual energy should not be repressed, but accepted and transformed. <Claim level="established" cite={["osho-sex-superconsciousness"]}>He posited that sex was the first step toward superconsciousness, and that religions prioritizing celibacy had created a deeply neurotic humanity.</Claim>

This publication led to widespread condemnation from orthodox leaders and the press, who dubbed him the "sex guru." However, it also significantly increased his profile, attracting a broader audience, including the first wave of Western seekers <Cite id="britannica-osho" />.

## Leaving Academia

By 1966, the demands of his traveling and speaking schedule, along with increasing friction with the university administration over his controversial public profile, led Osho to resign from his position at Jabalpur University. He transitioned fully into the role of a spiritual teacher, setting the stage for the establishment of his first major ashram.
