Between 1984 and 1985, federal, state, and county law enforcement conducted coordinated multi-agency investigations into the leadership of the Rajneeshpuram commune in Wasco County, Oregon <Cite id="usdoj-cr85-0150" />.

The investigations focused on multiple distinct areas: immigration violations and sham marriages to circumvent United States visa requirements, the illegal interception of telecommunications (wiretapping), weapons trafficking, and conspiracy to commit murder against state officials <Cite id="oregon-ag-investigation" />.

<Claim level="established" cite={['usdoj-cr85-0150']}>
  On October 23, 1985, a federal grand jury issued a 35-count indictment charging Osho and multiple associates with immigration conspiracy and false statements.
</Claim>

## The Charlotte Arrest and Alford Plea

Following the sealed indictment, Osho and a small group of disciples departed Rajneeshpuram on two chartered Learjets. On October 27, 1985, the aircraft landed in Charlotte, North Carolina for refueling, where federal agents placed him under arrest <Cite id="carter-2011" />.

On November 14, 1985, in U.S. District Court in Portland, Osho entered an <GlossaryTerm id="alford-plea" text="Alford plea" /> to two counts of immigration fraud, maintaining innocence of criminal intent while acknowledging the government possessed sufficient evidence to obtain a conviction <Cite id="usdoj-cr85-0150" />.

<Claim level="established" cite={['usdoj-cr85-0150', 'carter-2011']}>
  Under the terms of the plea agreement, Osho received a ten-year suspended sentence, five years probation, $400,000 in fines and restitution, and agreed to depart the United States immediately.
</Claim>

## The Division of Culpability

Following the collapse of the Oregon commune, deep disputes persisted regarding the degree of Osho's personal knowledge and complicity in the illegal actions directed by Ma Anand Sheela <Cite id="fitzgerald-1986" />.

<Claim level="disputed" cite={['sheela-dont-kill', 'carter-2011']}>
  While Sheela claimed that all clandestine activities were conducted with Osho’s explicit consent, federal prosecutors determined that evidence directly linking Osho to the violent conspiracies was insufficient for criminal indictment.
</Claim>

The federal court record concluded that while Osho fostered an environment of total isolation and obedience, the criminal acts of bioterrorism and attempted murder were carried out under Sheela's direct operational control.
