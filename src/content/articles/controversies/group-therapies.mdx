The Pune ashram (1974–1981) was famous not just for meditation, but for its intensive, often extreme group therapies. Drawing heavily from Western psychology—specifically Human Potential Movement practices like Primal Scream Therapy, Gestalt therapy, and <GlossaryTerm id="encounter-group" text="encounter groups" />—Osho integrated these with Eastern meditation practices <Cite id="carter-2011" />.

While many participants reported profound emotional breakthroughs, the execution of these therapies was deeply flawed and often unethical.

## The Push for Catharsis

Osho taught that modern humans were too repressed—emotionally and sexually—to simply sit in silent meditation. He argued that a period of violent catharsis was necessary to break through layers of social conditioning.

<Claim level="established" cite={['carter-2011', 'milne-1986']}>
  Therapy groups at the ashram were designed to strip away defenses rapidly, often involving
  physical confrontation, screaming, and forced emotional breakdown
</Claim>
.

## Boundary Violations and Abuse

The lack of professional oversight and ethical boundaries led to significant harm.

<Claim level="established" cite={['milne-1986', 'vanderKolk-2014']}>
  Documented accounts from ex-disciples and therapists describe sexually invasive practices and
  physical violence
</Claim>
. Therapists—who were appointed by Osho and treated as unquestionable authorities—often encouraged
or forced sexual contact between participants under the guise of "breaking repression."

There was no informed consent process, no screening for pre-existing trauma, and no psychological aftercare for participants who were destabilized by the intense experiences <Cite id="lalich-langone-2006" />.

## The Psychological Toll

Modern psychology understands that the surrender of will to an authority figure, combined with the breaking down of psychological defenses, creates patterns identical to trauma bonding <Cite id="lalich-langone-2006" />.

Many former sannyasins required years of therapy to recover from the PTSD and attachment disorders developed during their time at the ashram <Cite id="icsa-research" />. While Osho correctly identified that emotional catharsis can be helpful, the implementation at the Pune ashram demonstrated a dangerous disregard for human vulnerability.
