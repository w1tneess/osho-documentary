In 1974, after years of traveling and operating out of a Bombay apartment, Osho relocated to Pune. With the financial backing of Catherine Venizelos (Ma Yoga Mukta), the movement purchased two adjoining properties in Koregaon Park <Cite id="carter-2011" />.

This location became the "Shree Rajneesh Ashram," the movement's first major international headquarters.

## The Magnet for Western Seekers

The Pune ashram quickly grew from a modest Indian spiritual center into a booming international commune. By the late 1970s, it was receiving over 30,000 international visitors annually <Cite id="britannica-osho" />. 

<Claim level="established" cite={["urban-2015"]}>The ashram specifically attracted educated, middle-class Westerners, including psychologists, doctors, artists, and actors, who were dissatisfied with traditional Western religion and drawn to Osho's intellectual synthesis of mysticism and psychotherapy.</Claim>

Osho adopted a daily routine that defined the ashram's culture: a morning discourse (alternating between Hindi and English), and an evening *darshan* where he answered personal questions and initiated new followers into <GlossaryTerm id="sannyasin" text="sannyas" />. Initiates were required to wear orange or red clothing (the colors of the rising sun) and a *mala* (beaded necklace) bearing Osho's picture.

## The Therapy Groups

What set the Pune ashram apart from other Indian ashrams was its massive, institutionalized focus on Western humanistic psychology. Osho invited leading therapists from centers like Esalen to lead groups at the ashram.

The ashram offered dozens of therapy groups, combining gestalt, bioenergetics, primal scream, and psychodrama. The most controversial were the "Encounter" groups <Cite id="milne-1986" />.

## Tensions and Departure

Despite the financial success of the ashram—which operated a massive publishing house, a bakery, and various craft industries—its rapid growth caused severe friction with the local Pune community. 

Conservative Indian society was deeply offended by the influx of Westerners, the rumors of sexual promiscuity, and Osho's harsh criticisms of Indian politicians, particularly Prime Minister Morarji Desai. In response, the Indian government canceled the ashram's tax-exempt status and refused visas to foreign visitors <Cite id="carter-2011" />.

Coupled with an assassination attempt on Osho in 1980 by a fundamentalist Hindu, and Osho's rapidly declining health (which his doctors attributed to asthma, diabetes, and back problems), the movement's leadership—now spearheaded by Ma Anand Sheela—began searching for a new home outside of India.

In May 1981, Osho entered a period of public silence. On June 1, 1981, he left India for the United States, effectively ending the first Pune era.
